    Prediction model

    Hierarchical Bradley–Terry

    s = x · w + EV(x) + b

    Each species has a strength s. In a decisive duel the chance the left species wins is the logistic of sleft − sright. The linear piece x·w gives every input column its own weight. EV(x) is a two-layer network: those same columns are the inputs, each hidden unit has a bias and a weight on every input to it, and a readout turns the last layer into one number. The offset b belongs to that species alone.

    A tie counts as a half-win each way and enters the fit. A skip stays in the history so it can be undone, and it stays out of the fit. Weights and offsets are the mode of a normal prior plus that likelihood, found by alternating Newton steps. The ± beside a weight is its Laplace posterior standard deviation. It starts at the prior width and shrinks as matchups speak to that column.

    A species you have barely ranked stays near the strength its categories predict, because the prior pulls b toward 0. Rankings lists the full strength s for every species. A positive weight means species with that column on have been winning your duels.

    Linear weights

    Every trait category is a block of columns in x. Each column has its own learned weight.

    Network

    Inputs are those same columns. Hidden 1 is 8 tanh units, each with a bias and incoming weights. Hidden 2 is 4 tanh units. EV is the readout. Strength is s = x·w + EV(x) + b.
